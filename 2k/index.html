1. The short version

  1. Most of your rowing should be easy. A 2k is roughly 80% aerobic, so building your aerobic engine matters more than anything else.
  2. Do two hard sessions a week: one at 2k pace (short intervals) and one at threshold (longer intervals). Everything else is easy, steady rowing.
  3. Easy means easy: you can hold a conversation, at 18–22 strokes per minute. Rowing easy days too hard leaves you too tired for the key sessions.
  4. Use a drag factor of 110–135 and keep it the same every time. A heavier stroke doesn't make you fitter. It just tires your legs sooner.
  5. Log every session. When a workout comes around again, try to beat last time's average split.
  6. Test your 2k every 6–12 weeks, not every week.
  7. On test day, don't go out too fast. Settle on your target split by 300 m, hold it through the middle, and sprint the last 150–300 m.
  8. Take at least one full rest day a week. Your body gets fitter while you recover, not while you row.

2. Set up the erg

Drag factor

Use a drag factor of 110–135: toward 110 if you're lighter, toward 135 if you're heavier. A higher drag factor makes each stroke heavier but doesn't make you fitter. It tires your legs before your heart and lungs get a proper workout. Even Olympic rowers use only about 112–130.

  • Use the same drag factor every time, in training and in tests, so your splits are comparable.
  • To see it on the PM5: Main Menu → More Options → Display Drag Factor, then row a few strokes.

Feet and monitor

  • Set the foot stretcher so the strap goes across the widest part of your foot (about where the lowest shoelace is). Pull the strap tight enough that your feet don't slide.
  • Set up interval workouts with Select Workout on the monitor or with the ErgData app. ErgData uploads your results to the free Concept2 online logbook.

3. Technique basics

Good technique makes the same effort go faster and keeps your back happy. Watch the short videos on Concept2's technique page at least once. They explain it better than text can.

  • Drive: legs → body → arms. Push with the legs first, arms straight until the handle passes your knees. Swing the body back as the legs finish, then pull the arms in. Brace your core so the leg push goes into the handle.
  • Recovery: arms → body → legs. Arms out, lean forward from the hips, then bend the knees and slide forward.
  • Push hard, come forward slowly. The recovery should take about twice as long as the drive.
  • Finish: legs flat, lean back slightly, handle to the bottom of the ribs, elbows past the body, wrists flat.
  • Catch (front of the stroke): shins about vertical, arms straight, body leaning slightly forward. Don't over-reach.
  • Stay relaxed: hold the handle lightly and keep your shoulders down.

Rowers use five training zones. Most of your time should be in the first two.

  • On easy days, how it feels beats the pace target. If your breathing or heart rate says it's too hard, slow down.

7. The workouts

Each key session rotates through three workouts: rotation week 1, then 2, then 3, then back to 1. The rotation and pace targets come from the Pete Plan. Each time a workout comes around again, try to beat your average split from last time.

Rules for every interval session

  • Warm up for about 10 minutes: start easy, build to moderate, and finish with a few short bursts at the session's pace.
  • Rest between reps means very easy rowing or sitting still.
  • The first time you do a workout, row every rep except the last at the target split, then go as hard as you can on the last one. Write down the average split. That's the number to beat next time.
  • When you add a rep (or make a piece longer), keep the same split as last time.

UT2 (easy steady), all other rowing days

  • 30–60 minutes at your UT2 pace, 18–22 strokes per minute. Long UT2 is 45–75 minutes.
  • You can split it up, for example 3 × 15 minutes with a short break.
  • It should feel almost too easy. That's correct. Use the time to work on technique.

UT1 (low-rate firm steady), in the 5–6 day schedule

  • Pick one: 30 minutes at 20 strokes per minute, 2 × 20 minutes at 22, or 3 × 10 minutes at 22–24, with 5:00 easy between pieces.
  • The low rate is the point: long, strong strokes with as much power per stroke as you can.

Strength, 1–2 times a week

  • Compound lifts: front squats, deadlifts, pendlay rows or pull-ups, presses and core work.
  • Do it on a rest or easy day, not the day before a key session.

8. 12-week plan

This puts the weekly schedule on a timeline, ending in a 2k test.

WeekPhaseWhat to do
1Baseline Replace the speed session with a 2k test. Row it controlled: even pace, then finish strong. Use your time to find your paces in the table. The rest of the week is easy.
2–4Base Start the rotation (weeks 1, 2, 3). Use the shorter versions of the workouts if you're new.
5–7Build Go through the rotation again with the full-length workouts. Beat your splits from weeks 2–4.
8–10Build Go through the rotation again and beat weeks 5–7. Optional: replace the week 8 speed session with a 2k test and update your paces.
11Sharpen Easy rowing as normal. Key sessions: 8 × 500 m and 4 × 1000 m. Use the first strokes of each rep to practise your start and settle on your target 2k split quickly.
12Taper and test Row about half your usual amount, mostly easy. Do a couple of hard pieces of 60–90 seconds early in the week, then only 20–30 second bursts in the last few days. Keep it to about 300 hard strokes in total for the week. Rest or row easy the day before, and test at the end of the week.

After the test, take 2–3 easy days, update your paces with the new time and start again from week 2.

9. Test day

Pick a target

  • Target split = target time ÷ 4. For example, a 7:00 2k is a 1:45.0 split.
  • For a realistic target, use your training: about 3 seconds slower than your 8 × 500 m average, or about 1 second faster than your 4 × 1000 m average.
  • For your first tests, go for even splits, or start slightly slower than target and finish faster (a "negative split").

Before you start

  • Take a rest day or an easy day before. Sleep, eat and drink as usual, and don't try anything new on test day.
  • Check the drag factor and set it to your usual number.
  • Set up a 2000 m single-distance workout on the monitor.

Warm-up (about 20 minutes)

  1. 7–10 minutes of light steady rowing, gradually pushing harder. Focus on good strokes, not speed.
  2. 3 × 10 strokes at 24, 28 and 32 strokes per minute, with at least 20 easy strokes between them.
  3. 1 × 20 strokes at race pace and race rate.
  4. A few minutes easy, then go. You want to be warm, not tired.

Race plan

WhereWhat to do
Start (first 5–15 strokes)A few hard, quick strokes to get the flywheel going, then lengthen out.
Up to 300 mSettle on your target split and race rate. The numbers jump around for the first 200–250 m, which is normal.
300–1000 mHold your target split. Think about one stroke at a time, and don't do maths in your head.
1000–1500 mThis is where it starts to hurt, and where personal bests are won or lost. The third 500 is often 0.5–1 second slower than the others. Keep the strokes long and don't panic.
Last 500 mStart your sprint 300–150 m from the end: rate up, split down. Only go when you're sure you can hold it to the finish.

The most important rule: don't go out too fast. The first 500 m always feels easy. Going out too fast costs you more in the second half than it saves at the start.

Afterwards, row 5–10 minutes very easy to cool down, then log your result and update your paces.

10. Tracking progress

  • Log every session: date, workout, average split, stroke rate and how it felt. ErgData sends it to the Concept2 logbook automatically, but a notebook works too.
  • Between tests, progress means beating your own numbers in the rotation. That's your main feedback.
  • If your resting heart rate in the morning is 5 or more beats above normal, take it easy: you may be getting ill or not recovering. After a cold, wait until it's back to normal, do two easy days, then carry on.
  • If you miss sessions, don't try to cram them in. Make up one or two at most, then carry on with the plan.

11. Words and abbreviations

Split
Time per 500 m, the big number on the monitor. Lower is faster. Your 2k time ÷ 4 is your average split.
Stroke rate, spm
Strokes per minute. "r20" or "rate 20" means 20 strokes per minute.
8 × 500 m / 3:30 rest
Eight 500 m pieces with 3 minutes 30 seconds of rest after each one.
Drag factor
How heavy each stroke feels, measured by how quickly the flywheel slows down. See setup.
UT2, UT1, AT, TR, AN
The training zones, from easiest to hardest. See training zones.
Steady state
Continuous easy rowing, usually 30 minutes or more.
PB
Personal best.
Paul's Law
A rule of thumb: your split gets about 5 seconds slower each time the distance doubles.
Taper
Cutting training down before a test so you're fresh on the day.
